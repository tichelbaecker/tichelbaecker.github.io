intro: >
  My research spans three interconnected areas. I study the composition, emergence, and evolution of political elites and party organizations, the nature and causes of political polarization, and how conflict, violence, and extremist actors threaten liberal democracy. Across these areas, I combine large-scale archival data with experimental and observational research designs.

areas:
  - id: elites
    title: "Political Elites & Party Organizations"
    icon: fa-solid fa-users
    color: "#3d5a80"
    color_dark: "#7ca5cc"
    summary: >
      To better understand party organizations, I take a micro-level perspective and study who is running, why, and how this has changed over time and has affected party organizations. 
    questions:
      - question: "Who becomes a politician — and why?"
        answer: >
          Political representation begins with who enters politics in the first
          place. I study the composition of political elites — examining how
          individual characteristics, local context, and voter discrimination
          shape who stands for office and who succeeds. My work leverages newly 
          collected candidate data across all levels of government (national, state,
          local).
      - question: "How do parties select and recruit candidates?"
        answer: >
          Parties play a central role in the selection and recruitment of candidates. 
          I investigate how parties select and recruit candidates, how nomination 
          strategies respond to electoral incentives, and how the interplay 
          between political demand and supply determines the pool of representatives. 
          Drawing on novel candidate-level data from local elections, I trace 
          these dynamics at a granular level.
      - question: "How do local elites help new parties in establishing organizations?"
        answer: >
          My dissertation, *Taking Root*, asks how new political parties build
          durable local organizations. I show that new parties mobilize voters
          and establish roots in communities by recruiting local elites who
          leverage their existing ties — transforming party entry from a
          top-down directive into a bottom-up process.

  - id: polarization
    title: "Political Polarization"
    icon: fa-solid fa-arrows-left-right
    color: "#9b6a3c"
    color_dark: "#d4a06a"
    summary: >
      I study measures and drivers of political polarization, broadly understood. My work focuses on 
      affective polarization and a new polarization measure based on electoral behavior.
    questions:
      - question: "What drives polarization?"
        answer: >
          Citizens across democracies increasingly view political opponents
          with distrust and hostility. I study how people perceive and
          experience these divides — examining whether partisan animosity
          reflects genuine disagreement or distorted perceptions of the
          other side.
      - question: "How can we measure polarization?"
        answer: >
          Most work focuses on survey-based methods to understand polarization in the
          mass public. In work together with Lea Portmann, I study electoral behavior
          that can help us to understand the context in which de-polarized behavior 
          takes place.
      - question: "Who (de-)polarizes and why?"
        answer: >
          Previous work has shown that elites play a powerful role in shaping polarization
          of ordinary citizens. Building on this, I investigate which type of elites
          polarize and under which circumstances elites are able to reach out to voters
          that are ideologically distant.

  - id: democracy
    title: "Challenges to Liberal Democracy"
    icon: fa-solid fa-landmark
    color: "#4a7a5a"
    color_dark: "#7ab88e"
    summary: >
      Today liberal democratic institutions are challenged across the world. I study the
      rise of extremist actors, the effect of violence and war, and ways militant 
      democracies can counteract threats.
    questions:
      - question: "What fuels support for extremist actors in liberal democracies?"
        answer: >
          I study how and when far-right parties are able to attract support in liberal
          democracies, scrutinizing the role of domestic out-migration and gender imbalances.
          I also try to understand how and when far-right parties can build an increasingly
          loyal electorate.
      - question: "How do war and violence shape political behavior?"
        answer: >
          War and political violence leave lasting imprints on democratic
          politics. I study how exposure to conflict — whether through wartime
          casualties, political repression, or communal violence — reshapes
          citizens' political attitudes and behavior long after the fighting
          ends. I also study how political violence in established democracies shapes
          the composition of political elites.
      - question: "How can liberal democracies safeguard institutions?"
        answer: >
          Focusing on the case of Germany, I analyze the tools that militant democracies
          already have at their disposal to fend off challenges undermining
          state institutions. In particular, I focus on how state surveillance and
          the threat of disciplinary consequences can deter civil servants from joining
          extremist parties.
